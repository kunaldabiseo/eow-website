Kentucky Rules That Shape an Eye Malpractice Case

Since June 2019, Kentucky requires a certificate of merit to be filed with a medical negligence complaint (KRS 411.167). It states that the claimant consulted at least one qualified expert and that there is a reasonable basis to bring the claim. If the filing deadline does not leave time for that consultation, the certificate can be supplemented within 60 days of service. If requested medical records have not been produced, it is not due until 90 days after they are.

Timing matters too. Kentucky generally allows one year from the date the injury was discovered, or reasonably should have been discovered, to file the claim (KRS 413.140). Sending the case for an ophthalmology review early gives your team time to act on the opinion.

This is general information for attorneys, not legal advice. Your counsel should confirm how these rules apply to your case.

Kentucky Expert Ophthalmology Witness FAQs

Q

Does Dr. Lefkowitz work for both plaintiff and defense in Kentucky?

Yes. He reviews Kentucky eye cases for plaintiff and defense counsel, and gives the same independent opinion either way.

Q

Can he be the consulting expert for a Kentucky certificate of merit?

Yes. Under KRS 411.167 the attorney files the certificate of merit with the complaint, stating that a qualified expert was consulted and found a reasonable basis for the claim. Dr. Lefkowitz can review the records and serve as that consulting expert, and he can also provide affidavits.

Q

Does he need to travel to Kentucky?

Not for most of the work. Case review and depositions can be done by phone or video conference. He will travel to Kentucky for trial testimony.

Q

How soon should a Kentucky case be sent for review?

As early as possible. Kentucky generally gives one year from when the injury was discovered, or should have been discovered, to file a medical negligence claim (KRS 413.140). Your counsel should confirm the deadline for your case.
