How a District of Columbia case review works

  1. Send a short outline. The state, the procedure, the injury, the parties and any filing deadline in the District of Columbia. No records at this stage.
  2. Conflict check. Dr. Lefkowitz confirms he has no tie to any party before he reads anything confidential.
  3. Records review. You send the complete chart, the operative notes, imaging and test data. He reviews the care against what a reasonable ophthalmologist would have done at the time, and looks at causation.
  4. Verbal opinion first. You hear his view by phone, good or bad, before anything is put in writing.
  5. Report, affidavit or testimony. If you go ahead, he writes the opinion in the form the local rules call for, then testifies at deposition or trial, in person or by video.

District of Columbia expert ophthalmology witness FAQ

Can Dr. Lefkowitz act as an expert ophthalmology witness in the District of Columbia?

Yes. He reviews District of Columbia cases for both plaintiff and defense attorneys, gives deposition testimony in person or by video conference, and travels to the District of Columbia for trial.

What does the District of Columbia require before an eye malpractice case is filed?

No certificate of merit. A 90-day notice of intent is required before filing. (D.C. Code §16-2802) Check the current statute for your deadline.

Which standard do the District of Columbia courts apply to expert testimony?

Daubert (Motorola v. Murray, 2016). Dr. Lefkowitz bases each opinion on the records and on accepted ophthalmology practice at the time of the care, so it can be tested under that standard.

How quickly can he review a District of Columbia case?

Some cases are time sensitive. Once the case is received he can offer a quick turnaround, and he will tell you at the start if your deadline is too tight.
